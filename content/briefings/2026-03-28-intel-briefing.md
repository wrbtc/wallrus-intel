## Bitcoin & Crypto

**BTC dropped to ~$66K** on $300M in long liquidations around quarterly options expiry. Down 48% from the late-2025 $126K peak.

**Wallrus take:** The narrative that Bitcoin is "digital gold" or a geopolitical hedge is empirically dead in this cycle. When Trump paused Iran strikes for 5 days, BTC spiked $3K on a short squeeze. When escalation resumed, it sold off with equities. BTC is trading as a high-beta risk asset with tight equity correlation — not as a store of value. The SEC/CFTC commodity classification (16 assets including BTC, ETH, SOL) is genuinely significant for long-term institutional rails, but right now institutional buyers are adding to positions slowly while retail is getting liquidated on leverage. The 1.7M+ BTC held by public companies (8% of supply) creates a structural floor, but that floor is lower than most Bitcoin maximalists admit. Until the Iran premium leaves oil and equities stabilize, BTC follows the S&P, not gold.

**Source bias note:** CoinDesk and crypto-native outlets frame the drop as "a buying opportunity." Macro desks frame it as "risk-off continues." The truth is mechanical: quarterly options expiry forced liquidations. Neither narrative is wrong, but neither is the full picture.

## Geopolitics & Oil

**The Iran war had three major escalations overnight, and only one is getting the headlines it deserves.**

**1. Houthis entered the war (March 28).** Ballistic missile barrage at Beersheba, Israel — intercepted. This is the event we've been watching. After 4 weeks of "Hour Zero" posture and zero attacks, the Houthis finally acted. The Bab al-Mandeb second-chokepoint scenario is now live. The question is no longer "will they activate?" but "will they resume Red Sea shipping attacks?" Their 2023-24 campaign diverted 2,000+ ships and proved they can functionally close a chokepoint. If they do it again while Hormuz is already down to 6 ships/day, the double-chokepoint scenario puts 25-27% of global petroleum flow at risk.

**Wallrus take:** Most outlets are covering the Houthi missile at Israel as a military event. The bigger story is what it signals about Red Sea shipping. A missile at Israel is symbolic — Houthi missiles get intercepted. But Houthi anti-ship attacks in the Bab al-Mandeb strait are operationally devastating and much harder to defend against. Watch for the first shipping attack, not the missile headlines.

**2. Iran struck US troops at Prince Sultan Air Base, Saudi Arabia.** 10-12 wounded, 2 seriously. USAF refueling aircraft damaged. Total US casualties since Feb 28: 13 KIA, 300+ wounded.

**Wallrus take:** This is underreported relative to its significance. US troops wounded on Saudi soil by Iranian missiles creates domestic political pressure that abstract "Hormuz closure" headlines don't. Every wounded soldier makes it harder for Trump to extend the energy strike pause again on April 6. The gap between "we're in talks" (Trump's framing) and "they're shooting at our troops" (reality) is widening. Watch for how this plays in US media over the weekend — if it breaks through, the April 6 decision calculus shifts toward resuming energy strikes.

**3. Iran blocked Chinese ships at Hormuz.** Two Chinese-flagged vessels turned away near Larak Island. China was supposed to be a "protected" party under Iran's selective tollbooth system.

**Wallrus take:** This is the most strategically significant of the three events and it's getting the least coverage. Iran alienating China — its biggest remaining economic lifeline — suggests either desperation, internal command confusion post-Tangsiri assassination, or a deliberate signal that the blockade is now absolute, not selective. If China retaliates diplomatically or economically, Iran loses its last major trade partner. If China acquiesces, it signals that Hormuz is genuinely closed to everyone. Either outcome is bearish for anyone expecting a quick resolution.

**Oil:** Brent holding above $106, WTI near $99. Brent-WTI spread at ~$13 — the widest in 13+ years. The spread itself tells you the market believes Middle East waterborne crude is structurally impaired, not temporarily disrupted.

**Diplomacy:** Iran demands Vance as sole negotiator, rejecting Witkoff and Kushner. Trump says talks are going "very well." Iran denies talks are occurring. The negotiation gap is publicly enormous — 15-point US demands vs 5-point Iranian counter-conditions that are functionally incompatible.

**Wallrus take on the diplomatic track:** Strip the press releases and look at the structure. The US demands include dismantling 3 nuclear sites and abandoning all proxies. Iran's counter-demands include war reparations and sovereignty guarantees. These positions are not "far apart" — they are from different planets. A framework agreement before April 30 requires one side to capitulate, not compromise. The 2.75% ceasefire-by-March-31 pricing on prediction markets is about right. The broader April 30 window at ~40% YES is overpriced given the structural gap.

**Source bias note:** US outlets amplify Trump's "talks going well" framing. Iranian state media amplifies "we will never surrender." Gulf outlets focus on shipping disruption. Israeli outlets focus on military operations. None of them are lying — they're each covering the part of the elephant they can see. The full picture is: kinetic operations are escalating while diplomatic posturing continues. These are not contradictory — both sides are fighting and talking simultaneously, which is normal in wars. The question is which track moves faster.

## AI

**Anthropic: "Claude Mythos" leaked** — described internally as a "step change" in capabilities with unprecedented cybersecurity risk disclosures. Under trial with early access customers.

**Wallrus take:** The leak matters more than the model. Anthropic's internal framing of "step change" and elevated safety concern suggests this is not incremental. The cybersecurity risk language implies capabilities that could be weaponized — likely autonomous coding, system interaction, or reasoning that approaches genuine problem-solving. If Mythos ships broadly, it reshapes the competitive landscape not just for AI companies but for any knowledge work that depends on human judgment being better than machine judgment. The timing — while Anthropic is simultaneously fighting a Pentagon blacklist in court — adds political complexity.

**Wallrus take:** OpenAI killing its video product to fund robotics is a bigger strategic signal than most coverage suggests. It means OpenAI believes the next value frontier is physical-world AI, not content generation. If they're right, the entire "AI will replace creative workers" narrative is a sideshow — the main event is AI that operates in the physical world. Google DeepMind's partnership with Agile Robots (20,000+ deployed units) points in the same direction. The robotics convergence is accelerating faster than the public discourse about AI suggests.

## Europe

**Spain flash CPI printed 3.3%** vs 2.3% prior — hot, energy-driven. Germany CPI due March 30. Swap markets now pricing 75% probability of an April ECB rate hike.

**Wallrus take:** Europe's inflation problem just got an Iranian accelerant, and the policy response is about to collide with recession risk. The ECB cut rates steadily from June 2024 through January 2026 on the assumption that inflation was beaten. Iran changed that in four weeks. Spain's print is the first hard confirmation that the oil shock is feeding through to consumer prices. If Germany prints hot on March 30, the ECB faces an impossible choice in April: hike into a recession (OECD just cut eurozone growth to 0.8%) or hold and watch inflation expectations de-anchor. Swap markets at 75% hike probability feel aggressive — institutional inertia at the ECB is real, and this would be the first hike after seven consecutive cuts. But the direction of travel has clearly reversed.

**G7 split on Iran** at the emergency foreign minister meeting. European allies pushing de-escalation while the US keeps military options open.

**Wallrus take:** The G7 "split" framing is oversimplified. What's actually happening is that Europe has no leverage. They can't stop the US from striking Iran, they can't reopen Hormuz, and they can't absorb the oil shock. Their de-escalation push is real but toothless. The meaningful European story is not diplomatic positioning — it's the economic damage. Every week Hormuz stays closed, European energy costs compound. The ECB's dilemma is a direct consequence of European strategic impotence on Iran.

## Global Macro & Markets

**S&P 500 fell 1.57%, Nasdaq down 2.15%.** Tech hit hardest — Nvidia -2.2%, Meta -4%. Year-to-date the S&P is down 5.8%.

**Fed holding at 3.50-3.75%**, projecting one cut in 2026. PCE inflation forecast raised to 2.7%.

**Wallrus take:** The Fed is trapped in the same box as the ECB but with more room to wait. The Iran oil shock is inflationary, which means cuts are off the table. But the economy isn't overheating domestically — inflation is being imported via energy. The Fed will do nothing until either the war ends (oil drops, cuts resume) or the economy breaks (recession forces cuts despite inflation). The one-cut projection for 2026 is the Fed's way of saying "we have no idea." Markets are pricing in the uncertainty, which is why equities sell off on escalation — not because the war directly hurts US companies, but because it removes the monetary policy safety net.

**Supreme Court killed IEEPA tariffs.** Trump pivoted to Section 301 investigations against 15+ countries.

**Wallrus take:** The tariff story is getting lost in the Iran noise, but it matters. The Supreme Court ruling means Trump can't use emergency powers for trade wars. Section 301 is slower, more targeted, and subject to more legal challenge. The practical effect: the 10% global tariff under Section 122 runs through mid-June, then faces legal headwinds. This is mildly positive for global trade but the market hasn't priced it in because everyone is watching oil.

**DHS shutdown at Day 42** — longest partial government shutdown in US history. House and Senate passed incompatible bills. Senate left for recess until April 13.

**Wallrus take:** This story is effectively over as a news event but not as a political one. The Senate is gone. The House bill is dead in the Senate. No resolution path exists before mid-April. The interesting thing is what isn't happening: there's no public pressure to end it. Trump's TSA pay executive order removed the most visible pain point (airport delays), which means the remaining DHS employees are suffering quietly. The shutdown will end eventually through exhaustion, not crisis — probably via a quiet deal in late April.
